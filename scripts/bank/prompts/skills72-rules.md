# Choosing one of the 72 skills

Pick the ONE skill whose "tests" line best names what a student must do to get the question right.
Judge by the task (what is asked, and what the choices differ in), never by the passage's topic.
When two skills seem to fit, use the rules below. If none fits well, pick the closest and give a low
confidence (under 0.5).

## Reading and Writing

Information and Ideas

- rw-main-idea: asks for the main idea, central claim or point of the whole text.
- rw-key-details: asks what the text says, states or indicates about a detail, a character or a
  person ("According to the text...", "Based on the text, ..."), literary passages included.
- rw-evidence-from-a-text: asks which QUOTATION (from a poem, novel, play, letter or essay) best
  illustrates or supports a claim.
- rw-evidence-from-research: asks which finding, result or observation, if true, would most directly
  support, weaken or challenge a hypothesis, claim or conclusion; no table or graph is needed.
- rw-reading-tables: the answer depends on data in a TABLE (completing a claim, supporting it).
- rw-reading-graphs: the answer depends on data in a bar graph, line graph, scatterplot or other chart.
- rw-completing-an-argument: "Which choice most logically completes the text?" where the blank ends a
  line of reasoning that is not mainly a report of a study's results.
- rw-inferring-from-findings: the same "most logically completes" task when the text reports a study,
  experiment, survey or measurement and the blank says what the results imply (or do not imply).

Craft and Structure

- rw-precise-word-choice: a blank to fill with "the most logical and precise word or phrase" when the
  choices are everyday words or phrases.
- rw-advanced-vocabulary: the same blank task when the choices (the right one above all) are less
  common, advanced words (for example "ephemeral", "conciliatory", "obdurate", "esoteric").
- rw-meaning-in-context: asks what a word or phrase that is already in the text "most nearly means"
  as it is used there.
- rw-text-structure: asks for the overall structure of the text.
- rw-main-purpose: asks for the main purpose of the text.
- rw-function-of-a-sentence: asks what an underlined sentence or portion does in the text as a whole.
- rw-comparing-two-texts: two texts (Text 1 and Text 2); asks how they relate, where they agree or
  differ, or what both authors would say.
- rw-responding-across-texts: two texts; asks how the author of one would most likely respond to a
  claim, finding or view in the other.

Expression of Ideas

- rw-notes-similarities-and-differences: a notes question whose stated goal is to emphasize a
  similarity, a difference or a contrast, or to compare two things.
- rw-notes-presenting-a-study: a notes question whose goal is to present, introduce, summarize or
  explain a study or research: its aim, method, findings or conclusion.
- rw-notes-writing-for-a-goal: a notes question with any other goal (emphasize a point, give an
  example, make a generalization, introduce a person or work to an audience, specify a detail).
- rw-transitions-adding-on: the CORRECT transition adds, gives an example, restates, clarifies,
  specifies or shows similarity (Moreover, Furthermore, For example, In other words, Indeed,
  Specifically, Similarly, Likewise, In fact).
- rw-transitions-contrast: the CORRECT transition contrasts or concedes (However, Nevertheless,
  Still, In contrast, Instead, Conversely, That said, Even so, Regardless).
- rw-transitions-cause-and-sequence: the CORRECT transition shows a result, cause or order in time
  (Therefore, Thus, As a result, Consequently, Hence, Accordingly, First, Then, Finally, Afterward,
  Subsequently, Meanwhile, Later, Previously).

Standard English Conventions: decide by what separates the right choice from the wrong ones.

- rw-sentence-boundaries: joining or separating two complete sentences (period, semicolon, comma
  plus and/but/so), fixing run-ons, comma splices and fragments.
- rw-linking-clauses: attaching a dependent clause or phrase to a main clause (although, because,
  which, who, an opening phrase), where the issue is how the dependent part joins.
- rw-essential-and-extra-details: commas, dashes or parentheses around extra (nonessential)
  information, appositives, names and titles ("the novelist Toni Morrison" against "Toni Morrison,
  a novelist,").
- rw-colons-and-dashes: a colon or a dash after a complete sentence to introduce an explanation, an
  example or a list.
- rw-lists-and-series: commas or semicolons between items in a list or series.
- rw-unneeded-punctuation: the right choice has no punctuation where the wrong ones put a comma,
  colon or dash (between subject and verb, verb and object, before a preposition or that-clause).
- rw-subject-verb-agreement: the choices differ in a singular or plural verb.
- rw-pronoun-agreement: the choices differ in a pronoun (it, they, its, their, this, these, one).
- rw-verb-forms: the choices differ between a full verb and an -ing, to- or past participle form
  (does the sentence need its main verb here?).
- rw-verb-tense: the choices are full verbs in different tenses or aspects.
- rw-modifier-placement: an opening describing phrase, and the choices differ in what comes right
  after it (dangling or misplaced modifiers).
- rw-plurals-and-possessives: the choices differ in apostrophes, plural or possessive forms, or
  contractions (its/it's, their/they're, student's/students').

## Math

Algebra

- m-solving-linear-equations: solve a linear equation in one variable (fractions, brackets
  included), or find the value of an expression from one.
- m-number-of-solutions: when a linear equation has no solution, one, or infinitely many (for
  example, the constant that gives no solution).
- m-equations-from-words: write or pick the linear equation (one or two variables) for a situation.
- m-slope-and-rate-of-change: find a slope or rate of change, or say what a slope or intercept means
  in context.
- m-graphs-of-lines: intercepts, a line's equation from a graph, a table or two points, the forms of
  a line's equation, which graph shows a line.
- m-parallel-and-perpendicular-lines: how the slopes of two lines relate.
- m-linear-functions: evaluate a linear f(x), read linear function notation, tables or models.
- m-solving-systems: solve two linear equations (find x, y or an expression in them).
- m-systems-in-context: write or interpret a system for a situation; systems with no or infinitely
  many solutions.
- m-linear-inequalities: solve, write or graph linear inequalities, in one or two variables.

Advanced Math

- m-expanding-and-factoring: rewrite polynomial expressions: expand, factor, combine, equivalent
  forms.
- m-rational-expressions: simplify, add, multiply or divide fractions that contain variables.
- m-exponents-and-radicals: exponent rules, roots, fractional exponents, equivalent exponential or
  radical expressions.
- m-solving-quadratics: solve a quadratic equation, or say how many real solutions it has.
- m-graphs-of-quadratics: a parabola's vertex, axis, intercepts and forms; quadratic models.
- m-exponential-functions: exponential growth and decay, and what each number in the model means.
- m-radical-and-rational-equations: solve equations with roots, variables in denominators or
  absolute values; spot false (extraneous) solutions.
- m-nonlinear-systems: where a line meets a parabola or circle, or two curves meet.
- m-polynomials: zeros, factors, remainders and the graph's shape for polynomials (degree 3 and up
  above all).
- m-functions-and-transformations: function notation, composition, shifting or reflecting a graph,
  and other nonlinear function questions not covered above.

Problem-Solving and Data Analysis

- m-ratios-and-proportions: ratios, rates, proportional relationships and scaling.
- m-units-and-conversions: changing units, including speed and density.
- m-percentages: percent of, percent change and repeated percent change.
- m-mean-median-and-spread: mean, median, mode, range, standard deviation, and how data changes
  move them.
- m-reading-data-displays: histograms, box plots, dot plots and frequency tables of one variable.
- m-scatterplots: scatterplots, lines or curves of best fit, predicting from a model.
- m-probability: probability, conditional probability, two-way tables.
- m-samples-and-margin-of-error: what a sample can say about a population; margin of error.
- m-studies-and-experiments: study design, random selection or assignment, when a result can be
  generalized or shows a cause.

Geometry and Trigonometry

- m-area-and-perimeter: area and perimeter of flat shapes, composite ones included.
- m-volume-and-surface-area: volume and surface area of prisms, cylinders, cones, pyramids, spheres.
- m-lines-and-angles: angles made by parallel lines and a transversal, vertical angles, angles on
  a line.
- m-triangles: angle sums, similar or congruent triangles, isosceles and equilateral triangles.
- m-right-triangles: the Pythagorean theorem and special right triangles.
- m-trigonometric-ratios: sine, cosine and tangent, and sin x = cos(90° − x).
- m-circles: arcs, sectors, central and inscribed angles, circumference and area (in degrees).
- m-circle-equations: the equation of a circle in the xy-plane, its center and radius.
- m-radians: radian measure, radians and degrees, the unit circle.
