# Higgsfield concepts, for discussion

Status: ideas only. Nothing here has been generated and no credits have been spent.
Balance on 2026-10-20: 110 credits, Plus plan.

## 1. What the current plan makes, and its weakness

The brief (`2026-10-20-reform-brief.md`, Part 6) plans nine stills and four
short loops. All of them are empty rooms: a desk at blue hour with a green lamp,
the same desk on test morning, a kitchen table, a school hallway, a front
hallway with a packed bag. Each loop has one small movement, like steam from a
mug or car headlights crossing the ceiling. The style rules ban people, faces
and hands.

The plan is safe and consistent, but it runs against two things you have
already said:

- You found the earlier video depressing and asked for smiling people.
- You want Lando Norris and Jesko Jets energy. Empty rooms with steam are calm,
  not alive.

So the "no people" rule is the first thing to decide.

## 2. Rules any concept must keep

- No generated person is ever presented as a Karman student, parent or tutor.
  That means no names, no scores and no quotes next to them, so the images can
  never read as testimonials.
- The SAT is digital, so there are no bubble sheets and no paper test booklets.
  Students sit the test on a laptop or tablet in the Bluebook app. Scratch
  paper and a calculator are real; answer sheets are not.
- No College Board or Bluebook logos, and no readable screens.
- Colours stay in the palette: bone, night navy, program green, oxblood and
  ochre.
- No visible play button, and nothing that fills up as you scroll (rules 37
  and 38).

## 3. Concepts, from safest to boldest

**A. Seasons through one window.** Keep the desk from the current S1, but make
one version per cohort season: snow outside for the March cohort, rain and
blossom for May, early summer light for June. The homepage hero would match
whichever cohort is open, so the site would change with the calendar.
_Model: Nano Banana Pro, an edit of S1 (unlimited mode on Plus)._

> Edit the reference photograph. Same camera, desk, lamp and wall. Outside the
> window it is now late April: soft rain, a blossoming tree across the street,
> wet pavement reflecting warm windows. Everything inside stays unchanged.

**B. The cast.** Two or three fictional teenagers and one parent, kept
consistent across the whole site so the site tells one story: the first live
class (laughing at something on the laptop), the kitchen table with a parent
reading the weekly update, and leaving the test centre into daylight. The mood
is warm and confident, like a cast in an ad campaign.
_Models: Soul Cast for consistent faces, Soul 2.0 for stills (unlimited mode),
Cinema Studio Video 3.0 for motion._

> A 16-year-old student at a kitchen table on a weekday evening, laughing at
> something on a laptop angled away from camera, headphones around the neck,
> pencil behind the ear, a parent in soft focus in the background drying a mug.
> Warm practical light, deep navy shadows, documentary feel, 35mm, natural
> skin, no logos, no readable screen.

**C. The object (the Lando helmet idea).** The Lando Norris site is built around
one 3D object you can turn. Karman's equivalent would be a physical version of
the Folded Line K, as folded bone paper with a green edge or as brushed brass.
The loader's mesh would resolve into it, and it would turn slowly as the page
scrolls (the motion stays in the object, with nothing filling up).
_Models: Meshy 6 or Hunyuan 3D text-to-3D, which output a 3D file the site can
render live._

> A sculptural letter K made from a single folded strip of heavy bone-white
> paper, crisp creases, one edge painted deep forest green, standing on its own,
> museum-object quality.

**D. The question that comes back.** A metaphor for Daily 10's spaced review.
A sheet of scratch paper with one problem circled in pencil folds itself into a
paper plane, glides out of frame, and lands back on the desk.
_Model: Seedance 2.0 (unlimited mode) or Kling 3.0, start and end frame on the
same desk._

> Locked-off shot of a desk. A sheet of scratch paper with one loosely
> pencil-circled line folds itself into a paper plane, lifts, glides out of
> frame left, and a moment later glides back in from the right and settles
> where it started. Real-time, no people, no readable writing.

**E. Test morning, as a short film.** A sequence of four to six cuts for the
close of the homepage: an alarm at 6:30, a packed bag by the door, a parent's
car at a curb in grey light, a hallway, and the doors opening into daylight.
The page cuts between shots at points as you scroll, like a film title
sequence, instead of using one loop.
_Model: Cinema Studio Video 3.0 with multi-shot, 10 to 15 seconds._

**F. A tutor at work.** Over the shoulder, a pen on a tablet circles a
mistake, and the screen is soft and unreadable. Used on the private tutoring
page and in the 1:1 review section.
_Model: Soul Cinema still, then Kling or Seedance motion._

**G. Portal welcome moments.** Two- to three-second loops for the student,
parent and tutor portals: a lamp switching on, a door opening, a notebook
opening. These play once on first sign-in.

**H. Share cards.** Images for links shared in texts and social posts, one per
cohort date, with the date set in type.
_Model: GPT Image 2 (renders text well, unlimited mode)._

the Generate button, so confirm each one there before running it.
