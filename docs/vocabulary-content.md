# Vocabulary content review

Reviewed 2026-10-08. This is an editorial SAT-preparation resource, not an official College Board vocabulary list or a measured test-frequency ranking. The daily answer set and the word-part deck are separate datasets.

## Daily word game

The expanded answer pool contains **84 distinct words**: 73 source-reviewed choices from released College Board practice tests 4–10 and 11 earlier general-academic starters. One of the original 12 starters, _tenuous_, overlaps the verified group and uses its reviewed practice-test record in new games. The 73 reviewed records include 65 exact answer-choice spellings and 8 explicitly normalized dictionary lemmas whose inflected forms appeared in the choice. All 73 have a checked Merriam-Webster entry, a selected part of speech, an original concise definition paraphrase, a sense caution, and a PDF page link. The full source is [`docs/vocabulary-source/reviewed-daily-words.json`](vocabulary-source/reviewed-daily-words.json), SHA-256 `0aca436362791691921fcf0ff90256787889630690ba0d00bb0313bc57ae325a`; its [evidence report](vocabulary-source/reviewed-daily-words-evidence.txt) and [validation summary](vocabulary-source/reviewed-daily-words-validation.json) are alongside it. `node scripts/vocabulary/build-daily-deck.mjs` checks those records and generates the smaller client answer deck.

The selected 73 words appeared among answer choices, including distractors. The evidence does **not** establish appearance on an administered SAT, frequency, future appearance, or College Board endorsement. Source review was AI-assisted editorial work rather than independent human sign-off. The other 11 starters have dictionary support but no practice-test occurrence claim. The game starts with letters only and reveals the selected meaning, part of speech, caution, and available source links after a win or loss. It supports spelling and word familiarity; it does not reproduce the SAT's contextual word-choice task. Difficulty is editorial, not psychometrically measured. The 84 answers each appear once per UTC cycle before repeating, in an ID-derived order; a 15-letter board scrolls horizontally on narrow screens.

The records link to the consulted [Merriam-Webster dictionary](https://www.merriam-webster.com/) entries: [abate](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/abate), [austere](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/austere), [tacit](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/tacit), [oblique](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/oblique), [erudite](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/erudite), [laconic](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/laconic), [mitigate](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/mitigate), [tenuous](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/tenuous), [salient](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/salient), [assuage](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/assuage), [pliable](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/pliable), and [lucid](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/lucid).

The separate accepted-guess lists contain **120,327 five- to fifteen-letter lowercase spellings**. They are filtered from the official [SCOWLv2 `en_US-large` plain word list](https://github.com/en-wl/wordlist-diff/blob/71d7dd07676edb60ade43552e10b41314b7e9287/en_US-large.txt), release `rel-2026.02.25`, commit `71d7dd07676edb60ade43552e10b41314b7e9287`, source SHA-256 `fa1f9a1382df724be887d3a5d2d743095e6f33fc0949ebb22415c1554bb42fa7`. All 84 separately reviewed answers are added to the lists where necessary. The [upstream word-data permission notice](https://github.com/en-wl/wordlist-diff/blob/71d7dd07676edb60ade43552e10b41314b7e9287/Copyright) expressly permits redistribution and sale of word lists created from SCOWLv2 with the specified copyright notice. The complete upstream notice is preserved in [`public/vocabulary/NOTICE.txt`](../public/vocabulary/NOTICE.txt), available beside the distributed lists and linked from the game; each list also carries the required copyright line. Source copyright-file SHA-256: `090575a131b4260926c7a6b30a90aca0f5db5fbb5c46778e0c5855227bf6ebc3`.

To regenerate, download the linked `en_US-large.txt` at the pinned commit, verify its SHA-256, then run `node scripts/vocabulary/build-guess-lists.mjs <downloaded-file>`. The generator verifies the source hash itself, excludes capitalized forms, digits, punctuation, non-ASCII spellings and other lengths, deduplicates the result, and includes the reviewed answers. The 5–15-letter counts are 6,982, 11,728, 16,982, 19,568, 19,015, 16,113, 12,018, 8,202, 5,145, 2,907 and 1,667 respectively. Some dialect, archaic or offensive terms may remain accepted guesses because the dictionary has not been individually screened. They are never daily answers and guesses are not published. The list checks spelling only, not suitability for teaching. All daily answers are present.

## Word-part flashcards

The authoritative reviewed inventory in `public/vocabulary/reviewed-word-parts.json` contains **415 sense-specific cards: 83 prefixes, 50 suffixes, and 282 roots**. It has 401 distinct front spellings within the three categories and 14 additional sense or grammatical-role cards. The 149 recorded variant occurrences are related spellings within cards, not extra cards. Source SHA-256: `f9ec3e8686a005da389099baed13d765350ccf079cb5f23dd6bcbe66454f1c1e`; source size: 546,876 bytes.

Each source record retains a stable ID, literal front, meaning/back, variants, sense links, caution, level, relevance rationale, and consulted source evidence. The current `source_checked` status means the selected word-part sense is supported by the linked references; it does not certify every illustrative whole-word etymology or SAT frequency. The visible card shows only the word part on its front and only the meaning on its back. Optional notes after flipping expose variants, cautions, and source links. Illustrative examples are omitted from the UI because their individual etymologies were not all independently audited.

The generated client deck at `src/data/vocabulary/word-part-cards.json` projects these 415 records without examples. Regenerate it with `node scripts/vocabulary/build-word-part-deck.mjs`; the generator validates IDs, group, meanings, source links, and same-front sense relationships. The underlying inventory draws on the individual source links retained per record, including Merriam-Webster, Collins, Cambridge, the Online Etymology Dictionary, Membean root entries, Texas State University learning material, and [Reading Rockets' root and affix guide](https://www.readingrockets.org/topics/spelling-and-word-study/articles/root-words-suffixes-and-prefixes). Some pedagogical “roots” are technically combining forms; the interface categories describe how the part is studied, not a complete linguistic taxonomy. A matching letter sequence alone does not establish a root or determine a modern whole-word meaning.
