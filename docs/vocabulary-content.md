# Vocabulary content review

Reviewed 2026-10-08. This is an editorial SAT-preparation resource, not an official College Board vocabulary list or a measured test-frequency ranking. The daily answer set and the word-part deck are separate datasets.

## Daily word game

The 12 starter answers comprise 3 five-letter, 8 seven-letter, and 1 eight-letter words. Each is a dictionary headword with a short original meaning paraphrase. The game starts with letters only and reveals the meaning after a win or loss; it gives no sentence or definition clue before play. The words repeat after 12 UTC days until a larger reviewed full-word answer bank is accepted. Difficulty is editorial: these are reusable academic words rather than an independently measured SAT-frequency sample. The starter bank still needs broader difficulty and register review before it can support stronger coverage claims.

The records link to the consulted [Merriam-Webster dictionary](https://www.merriam-webster.com/) entries: [abate](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/abate), [austere](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/austere), [tacit](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/tacit), [oblique](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/oblique), [erudite](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/erudite), [laconic](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/laconic), [mitigate](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/mitigate), [tenuous](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/tenuous), [salient](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/salient), [assuage](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/assuage), [pliable](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/pliable), and [lucid](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/lucid).

The separate accepted-guess lists contain 70,587 five- to eight-letter lowercase spellings from the local `/usr/share/dict/words` Webster's Second International (web2) list. `/usr/share/dict/README` describes its provenance and states that the supplier regarded its 1934 copyright as lapsed. The checked-in lists exclude capitalized forms, digits, punctuation, and other lengths; they include listed inflections and plurals. Some dialect, archaic, or offensive terms may remain accepted guesses because this source has not been individually screened. They are never daily answers and guesses are not published. The list checks spelling only, not suitability for teaching. All daily answers are present.

## Word-part flashcards

The authoritative reviewed inventory in `public/vocabulary/reviewed-word-parts.json` contains **415 sense-specific cards: 83 prefixes, 50 suffixes, and 282 roots**. It has 401 distinct front spellings within the three categories and 14 additional sense or grammatical-role cards. The 149 recorded variant occurrences are related spellings within cards, not extra cards. Source SHA-256: `f9ec3e8686a005da389099baed13d765350ccf079cb5f23dd6bcbe66454f1c1e`; source size: 546,876 bytes.

Each source record retains a stable ID, literal front, meaning/back, variants, sense links, caution, level, relevance rationale, and consulted source evidence. The current `source_checked` status means the selected word-part sense is supported by the linked references; it does not certify every illustrative whole-word etymology or SAT frequency. The visible card shows only the word part on its front and only the meaning on its back. Optional notes after flipping expose variants, cautions, and source links. Illustrative examples are omitted from the UI because their individual etymologies were not all independently audited.

The generated client deck at `src/data/vocabulary/word-part-cards.json` projects these 415 records without examples. Regenerate it with `node scripts/vocabulary/build-word-part-deck.mjs`; the generator validates IDs, group, meanings, source links, and same-front sense relationships. The underlying inventory draws on the individual source links retained per record, including Merriam-Webster, Collins, Cambridge, the Online Etymology Dictionary, Membean root entries, Texas State University learning material, and [Reading Rockets' root and affix guide](https://www.readingrockets.org/topics/spelling-and-word-study/articles/root-words-suffixes-and-prefixes). Some pedagogical “roots” are technically combining forms; the interface categories describe how the part is studied, not a complete linguistic taxonomy. A matching letter sequence alone does not establish a root or determine a modern whole-word meaning.
